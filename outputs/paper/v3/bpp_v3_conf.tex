\begin{abstract}
Rare autoimmune diseases such as primary biliary cholangitis, systemic sclerosis and myasthenia gravis have few approved drugs, so computational repurposing for them must work \emph{zero-shot}: it must rank drugs for a disease whose treatments were never seen in training. We test three aims with \sysname, a path-based graph neural network in the BioPathNet family with an added transfer channel, on the PrimeKG knowledge graph under a strict leakage-free protocol that ranks all 7,957 drugs. (1) \emph{Source of transfer:} the model transfers knowledge from known diseases to never-seen ones (see (2)). When training data is scarce, the source matters: trained only on rheumatoid arthritis, lupus and multiple sclerosis, it ranks drugs for 12 held-out autoimmune diseases significantly better than when trained on label-matched non-autoimmune diseases (MRR \TrAEMrr{} vs.\ \TrActlEMrr; $p$\,\PwilTrAActlE). With the full graph, the source no longer matters: removing all autoimmune diseases changes nothing (\NoautoTargetsMrr{} vs.\ \MainTargetsMrr). (2) \emph{Accuracy:} for \TabModelTestAnyTen\% of 68 never-seen test diseases an approved drug is in the model's top 10 (random ranking: \RandExpAnyTenTest\%). It outperforms every baseline evaluated on the same split (MRR \TestMrr{} vs.\ \KnnTestMrr; $p$\,\PwilTestKnn). (3) \emph{Components:} path reasoning carries the accuracy, and the transfer channel adds stability. Unlabelled top candidates have registered trials \ValTrialModel\% of the time (random drugs: \ValTrialRand\%), and six drugs approved after the graph's snapshot rank at a median of \TempPostMedRank{} of 7,957. The model is a candidate-prioritisation tool for expert review, not a prescribing tool.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Drug repurposing finds new uses for approved drugs, whose safety profiles are already known, and is therefore faster and cheaper than de~novo development~\cite{pushpakom2019repurposing}. The need is greatest for rare diseases: as noted in~\cite{huang2024txgnn}, only 5--7\% of the more than 7,000 known rare diseases have an FDA-approved drug. Autoimmune disorders together affected 10.2\% of a 22-million-person UK cohort~\cite{conrad2023autoimmune}. Yet many of them, including primary biliary cholangitis (PBC), systemic sclerosis (SSc), Sj\"ogren syndrome, autoimmune hepatitis (AIH), myasthenia gravis (MG) and Crohn disease, have few disease-specific drugs and little curated data.

Biomedical knowledge graphs (KGs) link drugs, proteins, phenotypes and diseases, and link prediction on them can rank candidate drugs~\cite{himmelstein2017hetionet,bonner2022review}. For a disease without known treatments the task is \emph{zero-shot}~\cite{huang2024txgnn}. We use \emph{transfer} in this zero-shot sense: knowledge learned from diseases with known treatments is applied, without retraining, to diseases whose treatments the model has never seen. Autoimmune diseases share immune pathways, which suggests that the most useful source of that knowledge for rare autoimmune diseases could be the data-rich ones: rheumatoid arthritis (RA), systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and multiple sclerosis (MS). We test this through three aims:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Source of transfer.} Must the transferred knowledge come from related, data-rich autoimmune diseases, or does broad pharmacological knowledge suffice? We compare matched non-autoimmune training data and the full graph.
\item \textbf{Accuracy.} Does a path-based model beat simple, interpretable repurposing rules on diseases it has never seen?
\item \textbf{Components.} What do path reasoning, the transfer channel, the loss and the graph each contribute?
\end{enumerate}
We answer them with \sysname, a BioPathNet-style~\cite{hu2026biopathnet} Neural Bellman--Ford network (NBFNet)~\cite{zhu2021nbfnet}, evaluated under a strict leakage-free protocol, with seeds, confidence intervals and paired tests throughout.

\section{Related Work}
KG embeddings such as TransE, DistMult and RotatE~\cite{bordes2013transe,yang2015distmult,sun2019rotate} and relational GCNs~\cite{schlichtkrull2018rgcn} have been used for repurposing in COVID-19~\cite{zhang2021covidkg,morselli2021covid}, rare diseases~\cite{sosa2020rare}, KG-Predict and DREAMwalk~\cite{gao2022kgpredict,bang2023dreamwalk}. They learn a vector per node from its training edges, so they degrade for diseases without known drugs~\cite{huang2024txgnn,bonner2022kge}. TxGNN targets zero-shot repurposing with a GNN and disease-similarity metric learning~\cite{huang2024txgnn}. NBFNet represents a (disease, drug) pair by message passing that starts at the disease, which makes it inductive and explainable through paths~\cite{zhu2021nbfnet}. BioPathNet adapts it to biomedical KGs and reports stronger zero-shot indication ranking than TxGNN~\cite{hu2026biopathnet}. \sysname follows BioPathNet's design; our contributions are the transfer study, the evaluation protocol and a full-graph implementation that runs on a single 16\,GB GPU. Leakage is a known pitfall: Bri\`ere et al.\ show that redundant triples and random or cold-start splits overestimate repurposing performance in biomedical KG benchmarks~\cite{briere2026leakage,kapoor2023leakage}.

\section{Data and Protocol}
\textbf{Knowledge graph.} We use PrimeKG~\cite{chandak2023primekg}: 129,375 nodes (7,957 drugs, 17,080 diseases), 4,007,433 non-therapeutic edges of 27 relation types (each given an explicit inverse) and 42,631 therapeutic drug--disease pairs. The model predicts \emph{indications}. For the six targets, labels are indications plus off-label uses (PBC 1, SSc 5, Sj\"ogren 12, AIH 1, MG 7, Crohn 24 drugs). Given so few labels, the targets serve as case studies, and statistical claims rest on larger held-out sets.

\textbf{Leakage-free zero-shot protocol.} A held-out disease is removed together with its disease--disease neighbours (its \emph{family}). Without this rule, all 16 Crohn indications remained reachable through ``Crohn's colitis''. No therapeutic edge of a held-out family ever enters the message graph, and each training batch also hides its own query diseases' drug edges, so training mimics zero-shot inference. Every drug is ranked; no negatives are sampled. An earlier version of our pipeline violated these rules: test pairs were graph edges, and a no-learning degree score reached AUROC 0.972. All results below come from the rebuilt pipeline. The six target families and 68 random validation diseases are held out; 1,206 diseases with indications remain for training.

\textbf{Transfer conditions (Aim~1).} The test set $E$ contains the 12 diseases with indications in TxGNN's autoimmune area~\cite{huang2024txgnn} that are neither targets nor in the RA/SLE/MS families. $E$ and the six targets are held out in every condition, which differ only in their training diseases:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A}: the RA/SLE/MS families only (11 diseases, 161 indications), the original transfer design;
\item \textbf{A$'$}: three random sets of 11 non-autoimmune diseases, each matched to A disease by disease on number of indications;
\item \textbf{D}: all \TrDNTrain{} non-autoimmune diseases (all 123 autoimmune nodes removed);
\item \textbf{C$_E$}: all \TrCENTrain{} diseases except $E$.
\end{itemize}
A versus A$'$ asks whether autoimmune data helps when data is scarce; C versus D asks whether it still helps at scale. PrimeKG has no further autoimmune diseases to form an ``all autoimmune'' condition: beyond RA/SLE/MS, its autoimmune diseases \emph{are} the test area.

\section{Method}
\textbf{Path channel.} For a query disease $u$, node states start from an indicator, $h^{(0)}_v=\mathbb{1}[v=u]\,q$, with a learned query vector $q$, and pass through $L=6$ layers
\begin{align}
m^{(t)}_v &= \frac{1}{\deg v}\sum_{(x,r,v)} h^{(t-1)}_x \odot w^{(t)}_r \;+\; \mathbb{1}[v=u]\,q,\\
h^{(t)}_v &= h^{(t-1)}_v + \mathrm{ReLU}\big(\mathrm{LN}\big(W^{(t)}[h^{(t-1)}_v;\, m^{(t)}_v;\, a_v m^{(t)}_v]\big)\big),
\end{align}
with DistMult relation vectors $w_r$ and a PNA-style degree scaler $a_v$~\cite{corso2020pna}. An MLP over $[h^{(1)}_d;\dots;h^{(L)}_d;q]$ scores every drug $d$ in one pass.

\textbf{Transfer channel.} A small MLP over four leakage-free features adds a second score: drug popularity among training diseases, a disease-similarity $k$NN score ($k=20$, TF--IDF over disease proteins and phenotypes), shared drug-target/disease-gene count, and frequency among autoimmune training diseases. Each is computed with the query's family excluded.

\textbf{Training.} A multi-positive softmax over all drugs is minimised. Message passing uses 60 relation-wise sparse matrices with a custom autograd function that recomputes products in the backward pass, so memory scales with nodes, not edges: 68,226 parameters, 8.5\,s per step of 8 diseases and 5.8\,GB peak on an NVIDIA T4. Adam (learning rate $5\times10^{-3}$) runs with early stopping on validation MRR. Hyperparameters were fixed in advance, not tuned.

\textbf{Evaluation.} Every drug is ranked, and each known drug's rank is \emph{filtered} (other known drugs removed)~\cite{bordes2013transe}. We report, in order of interpretability: \emph{Any@10}, the share of diseases with at least one approved drug in the top 10; Hits@$k$, the share of known drugs in the top $k$; AUROC, including against hard (other autoimmune) and degree-matched negatives; AUPRC, next to its random level (the prevalence, about 0.001 here); and MRR~\cite{voorhees1999trec}. MRR is the mean of $1/\text{rank}$ over known drugs. On the test split, an MRR of \TestMrr{} corresponds to a median known-drug rank of \MedRankTest, with \RankOneShareTest\% of known drugs ranked first. AUROC is high even for popularity (\PopValidAuroc), so it is never used alone~\cite{saito2015prc}. A further 68 diseases form a \emph{test} split that is used neither for training nor for early stopping. Six non-neural baselines run on every protocol; the strongest is disease $k$NN, which transfers the drugs of the 20 most similar training diseases. CIs are bootstraps over diseases~\cite{efron1993bootstrap}. A difference counts as significant only if the paired bootstrap CI excludes zero \emph{and} a two-sided Wilcoxon test gives $p<0.05$~\cite{wilcoxon1945}.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Aim 1: transfer to rare autoimmune diseases}
Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer} shows the transfer curve.
\emph{Scarce data.} Trained only on the RA/SLE/MS families (A), \sysname reaches MRR \TrAEMrr{} on $E$, against \TrActlEMrr{} when trained on label-matched non-autoimmune diseases (A$'$). The paired difference of \DiffTrAActlE{} (CI \DiffLoTrAActlE{} to \DiffHiTrAActlE; Wilcoxon $p$\,\PwilTrAActlE; better on \WinsTrAActlE{} of 12 diseases) is significant. It also holds when each run's final checkpoint is used instead of the early-stopped one (\DiffTrAActlELast, CI \DiffLoTrAActlELast{} to \DiffHiTrAActlELast, $p$\,\PwilTrAActlELast). The rare targets point the same way (\TrATargetsMrr{} vs.\ \TrActlTargetsMrr; $p$\,\PwilTrAActlTargets, $n=6$). The disease-$k$NN baseline shows the same pattern (\TrAEKnnMrr{} vs.\ \TrActlEKnnMrr), and in this regime the model is only slightly ahead of it. With 11 training diseases, the transferable knowledge is essentially a drug-class prior. The RA/SLE/MS families are treated mostly with corticosteroids and TNF inhibitors, and the A-trained model ranks the same corticosteroids first for every target. That is a reasonable first guess for an autoimmune disease, but it is not disease-specific reasoning. To test whether path reasoning itself transfers in this regime, rather than only the drug-class prior carried by the transfer channel, we repeated A and A$'$ with paths only: \TrAPathsEMrr{} vs.\ \TrActlPathsEMrr{} (difference \DiffTrAActlPathsE, CI \DiffLoTrAActlPathsE{} to \DiffHiTrAActlPathsE; $p$\,\PwilTrAActlPathsE).

\emph{Full scale.} Broad training data without any autoimmune disease (D) raises accuracy on $E$ to \TrDEMrr. Adding the autoimmune diseases (C$_E$, one seed) gives \TrCEEMrr{} (difference \DiffTrCEDE, $p$\,\PwilTrCEDE), with no measurable gain. On the rare targets, removing all autoimmune diseases likewise leaves MRR unchanged (\NoautoTargetsMrr{} vs.\ \MainTargetsMrr; \NoautoSeeds{} seeds each; $p$\,\PwilMainNoautoTargets).
Aim~1 is therefore answered in two parts. The model's zero-shot transfer works at every scale we tested (Aim~2). Its best \emph{source} depends on how much data is available: related autoimmune diseases when training data is scarce, and the broad pharmacological graph, which then makes autoimmune data unnecessary, at full scale.

\subsection{Aim 2: accuracy on unseen diseases}
Table~\ref{tab:main} gives every metric. On the selection-free test split, an approved drug is in the top 10 for \TabModelTestAnyTen\% of diseases, against \TabKnnTestAnyTen\% for disease $k$NN, \TabPopTestAnyTen\% for popularity and \RandExpAnyTenTest\% expected by chance. MRR is \TestMrr{} versus \KnnTestMrr{} (paired difference \DiffTestKnn, CI \DiffLoTestKnn{} to \DiffHiTestKnn; $p$\,\PwilTestKnn). On the 68 validation diseases (\MainSeeds{} seeds, sd \MainValidMrrSd) the gain over disease $k$NN is \DiffMainKnnValid{} MRR (CI \DiffLoMainKnnValid{} to \DiffHiMainKnnValid; $p$\,\PwilMainKnnValid). \sysname therefore outperforms every baseline evaluated on the same leakage-free split. On the six rare targets the gain points the same way for \WinsMainKnnTargets{} of 6 diseases but is not significant with $n=6$ ($p$\,\PwilMainKnnTargets).

\subsection{Aim 3: what each component contributes}
Path reasoning carries the signal. Paths alone reach \PathsValidMrr{}, tied with the full model ($p$\,\PwilMainPathsValid), while the transfer channel alone reaches only \PriorValidMrr. The transfer channel does not raise mean accuracy, but it reduces seed variance on the targets (sd \MainTargetsMrrSd{} vs.\ \PathsTargetsMrrSd). In single-seed ablations, the listwise loss beats NBFNet's self-adversarial BCE (\MainValidMrr{} vs.\ \BceValidMrr; $p$\,\PwilMainBceValid). A BioPathNet-style background graph without tissue-expression and drug--drug edges removes \BrgEdgeCutPct\% of edges at unchanged accuracy (\BrgValidMrr; $p$\,\PwilMainBrgValid) and shortens training steps by \BrgSpeedupPct\%.

\emph{Explanations.} Gradient-based path extraction~\cite{zhu2021nbfnet} shows the reasoning behind each prediction. For example, amifampridine reaches MG through its indication for Lambert--Eaton myasthenic syndrome, a related neuromuscular-junction disease. The paths also expose weak predictions: ciprofloxacin reaches Crohn through ``mouth disease'' and sinusitis.
\emph{Popularity bias.} Corticosteroids fill \ValSteroidShare\% and antibacterials \ValAntibioticShare\% of all top-20 slots, so the lists need expert filtering.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Our MRR values fall within the range BioPathNet reports on TxGNN's zero-shot disease areas (about 0.2--0.8)~\cite{hu2026biopathnet}. TxGNN reports AUPRC against one sampled negative per positive~\cite{huang2024txgnn}, whereas we rank all 7,957 drugs. We ran neither system on our split, so we make no head-to-head claim. Limitations: (i) the targets have 1--24 labelled drugs, so per-target metrics are noisy and can be lower bounds (AIH); (ii) the validation diseases also select the stopping epoch, which adds \SelGapMainMin--\SelGapMainMax{} MRR of optimism, far below the gain over disease $k$NN and confirmed by the selection-free test split; (iii) the transfer test set $E$ has only 12 diseases, and models trained on 11 diseases are unstable across seeds; (iv) TxGNN's autoimmune area also contains autoinflammatory diseases; (v) the test split has \TestSeeds{} seed so far, and literature titles were classified with AI assistance and need author verification; (vi) corticosteroids and antibiotics dominate the candidate lists; (vii) predictions are hypotheses for expert review, not treatment advice.

\section{Conclusion}
Path-based zero-shot transfer works for rare autoimmune diseases that the model never sees. When training data is scarce, related autoimmune diseases are the most useful source of that knowledge. \sysname outperforms every baseline evaluated on the same leakage-free split, recovers established therapies for several rare autoimmune diseases, and ranks drugs approved after its training data well above chance. At full scale, the broad pharmacological graph supplies the same knowledge, and autoimmune training data adds nothing measurable. Path reasoning drives the accuracy, and the transfer channel contributes stability. Code, splits and run logs are released.
